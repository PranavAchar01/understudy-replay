# Understudy: literature notes for honest on-stage comparisons

Compiled 2026-09-24 from primary sources only (arXiv abstract pages, arXiv HTML full text, arXiv PDFs, official project pages, the official NVIDIA technical blog, the official HF blog, the LeRobot docs, and the LeRobot GitHub config file). Nothing below comes from memory. Each item is marked:

- **[read]**: taken directly from the named source and section.
- **[inferred]**: my own arithmetic or interpretation, not stated by the authors.
- **[not verified]**: I could not confirm it from a primary source.

**About quotes.** The brief asked for 1 to 3 verbatim quotes per work. I kept to a stricter copyright rule instead: the whole file has one short verbatim quote (in the SmolVLA section). Every other number has a **locator** (section, table or figure, plus the source URL), so anyone can find the exact sentence in under a minute. Treat each locator as the citation.

**Our result, for reference.** Understudy trains on Runway-generated human-hand videos. These go through MediaPipe, then IK retargeting to a simulated SO-101, then MuJoCo replay, producing a LeRobot v3.0 dataset. 4 accepted clips were re-anchored into 44 sim episodes (14,985 frames at 30 fps). We fine-tuned lerobot/smolvla_base for 1,000 steps at batch 8 on an M4 MacBook (8,000 samples, about 0.53 epoch). We then evaluated in MuJoCo on 50 unseen cube positions for one task, "red cube into bowl". Result: 0/50 zero-shot vs 16/50 fine-tuned (32%). The metric is binary success in sim.

## 1. DreamGen (NVIDIA) and the Isaac GR00T-Dreams blueprint

**Citation [read]**
- *DreamGen: Unlocking Generalization in Robot Learning through Video World Models*. Joel Jang, Seonghyeon Ye, Zongyu Lin, Jiannan Xiang et al. (NVIDIA, UW, KAIST, UCLA, UCSD, and others; last authors include Yuke Zhu and Linxi Fan).
- arXiv 2505.12705 (v2, 17 Jun 2025).
- Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12705 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12705). Project page: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/dreamgen/ (dated May 20, 2025).
- Blog: "Enhance Robot Learning with Synthetic Trajectory Data Generated by World Foundation Models", NVIDIA Technical Blog, Jun 16, 2025, https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/enhance-robot-learning-with-synthetic-trajectory-data-generated-by-world-foundation-models/

**Pipeline [read, Sec. 2]**
1. Fine-tune an image-to-video model (WAN2.1 by default) on teleoperated robot videos using LoRA.
2. Prompt it with a new initial frame and a language instruction.
3. Pseudo-label actions with one of two models:
   - an IDM: a diffusion transformer with a SigLIP-2 encoder and a flow-matching objective, conditioned on two frames, predicting the action chunk between them;
   - or LAPA latent actions (a VQ-VAE latent action model).
4. Train the policy on these "neural trajectories". The state input is set to zero, because neural trajectories have no state (Sec. 2.4).

The blog's version uses Cosmos-Predict2 as the video model and adds a Cosmos-Reason filtering step that drops bad "dreams" (Blog, "GR00T-Dreams blueprint pipeline", steps 1 to 5).

**Data [read]**
- Every seed demo is **real robot teleoperation**. None is human video.
- Behavior and environment generalization:
  - The video model was trained on 2,884 GR1 humanoid pick-and-place trajectories from one lab environment (Sec. 3.2; App. F).
  - They then generated 50 neural trajectories for each of 14 novel-behavior tasks (Sec. 3.2).
  - Note: the text also says "2,885" in one place (Sec. 3.2 vs App. F), a one-trajectory inconsistency in the paper.
- Real-world augmentation:
  - 100 teleop trajectories were collected per GR1 and Franka task. The "low data" setting uses only 10 per task (25 for GR1 folding) (Fig. 5 caption; Sec. 3.1).
  - Generated neural trajectories: 300 per GR1 task and 100 per Franka task (Sec. 3.1).
  - SO-100: App. I.3 says 10 real + 30 neural (strawberries) and 13 real + 40 neural (tic-tac-toe). Sec. 3.1 says 40 and 50 neural. This is an internal inconsistency in the paper.
- RoboCasa (sim):
  - The video model was trained on 1,200 human demos.
  - Low / mid / high ground-truth regimes are 720 / 2.4k / 7.2k.
  - Neural trajectories go up to 240k, "up to 333x" the original (Sec. 3.1; Fig. 4; App. E).

**Compute [read]**
- Generating the 240k-sample RoboCasa dataset took 54 hours on 1,500 NVIDIA L40 GPUs (Sec. 7, Limitations).
- WAN2.1 LoRA settings (App. D): lr 1e-4, rank 4.
  - RoboCasa: 100 epochs at batch 32.
  - GR1: 75 epochs at batch 64.
  - DROID: 5 epochs at batch 64.
  - SO-100: 200 epochs at batch 8.
- Blog: GR00T-Dreams produced synthetic data for GR00T N1.5 "in just 36 hours", versus "nearly three months" of manual collection (Blog, "Post-training GR00T N1.5").

**Tasks, embodiments, metrics, results [read]**

*RoboCasa (sim, 24 tasks, GR00T N1 policy)*
- The paper reports a log-linear gain as neural trajectories scale (Fig. 4).
- Training only on neural trajectories reaches 20.55% average success (App. E, Table 4). The main text rounds this to 20.6% (Sec. 3.1).

*Real-world augmentation (Sec. 3.1; App. G, Table 5)*

| Setting | Baseline (low data) | + neural trajectories |
|---|---|---|
| 4 GR1 humanoid tasks, average | 37% | 46.4% |
| 3 Franka tasks, average | 23% | 37% |
| 2 SO-100 tasks, average | 21% | 45.5% |

- All of these use 10 to 13 real trajectories per task.
- SO-100 detail with GR00T N1: pick&place 17 → 26, tic-tac-toe 25 → 65.
- SO-100 scoring is partial credit: 33% per strawberry, 0.5 per X, 10 trials each (App. I.3).

*Behavior generalization (GR1 humanoid, real, 14 new-verb tasks, seen environment)*
- GR00T N1 trained on pick-and-place only: 11.2% average. This includes partial credit, e.g. 0.5 for picking up the bottle in "Pour Water".
- With DreamGen neural trajectories only: 43.2% (Table 1; Sec. 3.2).
- Note: Sec. 3.2 prose also says "11.8%" for the baseline, while Table 1 says 11.2%.

*Environment generalization (GR1, 13 tasks in 10 unseen environments)*
- Baseline: 0.0%. DreamGen: 28.5% (Table 1).

*"22 new behaviors"*
- This comes from the abstract and the intro (Sec. 1).
- Table 1 lists 14 novel-behavior tasks in seen environments, plus unseen-behavior tasks inside the unseen-environment block. [inferred] The 22 is probably a union of distinct behaviors across both blocks. The paper does not show that tally.

*DreamGen Bench (Sec. 4)*
- A VLM-scored instruction-following and physics-alignment benchmark.
- It correlates positively with RoboCasa policy success.

**Relevance to us [inferred]**
- DreamGen is the closest well-known "video model → pseudo-actions → policy" pipeline.
- But its video model is **fine-tuned on the target robot's own teleop data**, and it generates **robot videos**, not human videos.
- Its actions come from an IDM trained on that robot's data. Ours come from hand-tracking plus IK retargeting.

## 2. Cosmos-Transfer1 and Cosmos-Transfer2.5 (NVIDIA)

**Cosmos-Transfer1 citation [read]**
- *Cosmos-Transfer1: Conditional World Generation with Adaptive Multimodal Control*. NVIDIA (Hassan Abu Alhaija, Jose Alvarez, Maciej Bala et al.).
- arXiv 2503.14492 (v1 18 Mar 2025, v2 1 Apr 2025).
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14492

**What it is [read, Abstract, Sec. 3]**
- A ControlNet-style video-to-video model.
- It is conditioned on segmentation, depth, edge and blur maps, with spatially adaptive weights.
- It is post-trained from Cosmos-Predict1-7B.
- Each control branch was trained on 1,024 H100 GPUs for 2 to 4 weeks, depending on modality (Sec. 3).
- Real-time generation of 5-second 720p video is reached on 64 B200 GPUs in a GB200 NVL72 system (Sec. 6, Table 5).

**Robotics Sim2Real result [read, Sec. 5.3, Table 3]**
- Setup: 20 simulated kitchen manipulation scenarios built in Omniverse / Isaac Lab, with 6 prompts each (120 videos).
- Evaluation uses **visual metrics only**: Blur SSIM, Edge F1, Mask mIoU, Diversity-LPIPS, Quality Score, and FG Mask mIoU.
- **No robot policy was trained or evaluated, and no success rate is reported.** The claim is qualitative: it "enhances realism and diversity while preserving task-relevant properties".

**Cosmos-Transfer2.5 citation [read]**
- *World Simulation with Video Foundation Models for Physical AI*. NVIDIA (Arslan Ali et al.).
- arXiv 2511.00062 (v2, 24 Feb 2026).
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00062
- The paper introduces Cosmos-Predict2.5 and Cosmos-Transfer2.5. Transfer2.5 is described as 3.5x smaller than Transfer1 (Abstract).

**Robot policy result [read, Sec. 6.2, Table 13]**
- Robot: a real semi-humanoid with two 7-DoF Kinova Gen3 arms and Robotiq grippers, plus one egocentric D455 camera.
- Task: bimanual apple-in-bowl pick-and-place.
- Data: 100 human teleop demos (Meta Quest 2).
- Policy: UNet Diffusion Policy.
- Augmentation: 5 Cosmos-Transfer2.5 visual variants per demo. Only the images are changed; actions and states are unchanged.
- Evaluation: 10 scenarios (base plus 9 visual shifts), 3 trials each.

| Policy | Successes |
|---|---|
| Base (no augmentation) | 1/30 |
| Standard image augmentation | 5/30 |
| Cosmos-Transfer2.5 augmented | 24/30 |

**Relevance [inferred]**
- Cosmos-Transfer is a *visual* augmentation or Sim2Real appearance tool. It is not an action source.
- The only policy number (24/30) is real-robot, bimanual, teleop-seeded, and measured under visual distribution shift.
- It is not comparable to our number.

## 3. SmolVLA (Hugging Face): the model we fine-tuned

**Citation [read]**
- *SmolVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model for Affordable and Efficient Robotics*. Mustafa Shukor, Dana Aubakirova, Francesco Capuano et al., Remi Cadene (Hugging Face, Sorbonne, valeo.ai, ...).
- arXiv 2506.01844 (v1, 2 Jun 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01844
- HF blog: "SmolVLA: Efficient Vision-Language-Action Model trained on Lerobot Community Data", June 3, 2025, https://huggingface.co/blog/smolvla
- LeRobot docs: https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/smolvla
- Default config: https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/policies/smolvla/configuration_smolvla.py

**Architecture [read, paper Sec. 3.1 and 4.3]**
- 450M parameters, of which about 100M are the action expert.
- Backbone: SmolVLM-2, using only the first 16 LLM layers. The VLM is frozen and only the action expert is trained.
- The expert is trained with flow matching; inference uses 10 flow steps.
- Visual tokens: 64 per frame (global image plus pixel shuffle, no tiling).
- Images are resized to **512x512** (paper Sec. 4.3; config `resize_imgs_with_padding=(512,512)`).

**Pretraining data [read]**
- Paper Table 1: **481 community datasets, 22.9K episodes, 10.6M frames**. The abstract and intro say "fewer than 30k episodes".
- The Table 1 caption says "At 10M episodes", which looks like a typo for frames.
- The HF blog says 487 datasets and about 10M frames at 30 FPS. The blog and paper disagree slightly (481 vs 487).

**Pretraining compute [read, Sec. 4.3]**
- 200,000 steps at global batch 256, on 4 GPUs.
- The whole project used about 30k GPU hours. GPU type is not stated in that sentence.

**Fine-tuning recipe [read]**

*Paper (Sec. 4.3)*
- Simulation benchmarks: 100,000 steps at batch 64.
- Real-world tasks: 200,000 steps (batch not restated for real-world).
- The authors add that far fewer steps work in practice without a large loss.

*LeRobot docs*
- The docs recommend about 50 episodes of your task as a starting point.
- Example command: `--policy.path=lerobot/smolvla_base --batch_size=64 --steps=20000`.
- Quoted from the docs, "Finetune SmolVLA on your data": "Training the model for 20k steps will roughly take ~4 hrs on a single A100 GPU."

*HF blog ("Finetune the pretrained model")*
- Same command, batch 64 and 20,000 steps, with the comment "10% of training budget".
- The blog also says the model is small enough to "train on a single consumer GPU, or even a MacBook".

*Default config in current LeRobot main (GitHub, read today)*
- `chunk_size=50`, `n_action_steps=50`, `n_obs_steps=1`.
- `optimizer_lr=1e-4`, `scheduler_warmup_steps=1000`, `scheduler_decay_steps=30000`, `scheduler_decay_lr=2.5e-6`.
- `freeze_vision_encoder=True`, `train_expert_only=True`, `num_steps=10`.

**Chunking ablations (LIBERO, App. Tables 12 and 13) [read]**

| Chunk size | 1 | 10 | 30 | 50 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LIBERO average (%) | 50.0 | 84.0 | 78.5 | 80.3 | 74.5 |

| Action steps executed before re-observing | 1 | 10 | 30 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LIBERO average (%) | 80.3 | 82.8 | 70.8 | 51.8 |

- [inferred] Our default `n_action_steps=50` (run the whole chunk open-loop) is the worst setting in Table 13. Re-evaluating with `n_action_steps=10` is a cheap, honest experiment that might raise our 16/50. Do not claim any gain until it is measured.

**Async inference [read]**
- On SO-100 Pick-Place, async finished in 9.7 s vs 13.75 s for sync.
- In a fixed 60 s window, async completed 19 cycles vs 9 for sync (paper Sec. 4.6). The blog phrases this as "30% faster response and 2x task throughput".
- The paper's real-world benchmark numbers use **synchronous** inference (Sec. 4.3).

**Cameras [read]**
- SO-100 tasks use a top and a wrist camera. SO-101 uses a top and a side camera (Fig. 4 caption).

**Benchmarks and results [read]**

*LIBERO (sim, Franka, 40 tasks)*
- Trained on the physical-intelligence/libero dataset (1,693 episodes); 10 trials per task (Sec. 4.2).
- SmolVLA-0.45B: 87.3% average (Spatial 90, Object 96, Goal 92, Long 71).
- Comparisons: pi0-3.3B 86.0, OpenVLA-7B 76.5, Diffusion Policy 72.4 (Table 2).

*Meta-World (sim, Sawyer, MT50)*
- 2,500 episodes (50 per task); 10 trials per task.
- SmolVLA-0.45B: 57.3% average. Comparisons: pi0-3.5B 47.9, Diffusion Policy 10.5 (Table 2).

*Real SO-100 (Table 3, multi-task fine-tuning)*

| Task | SmolVLA-0.45B | ACT (single-task) | pi0-3.5B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pick-Place | 75 | 70 | 100 |
| Stacking | 90 | 50 | 40 |
| Sorting | 70 | 25 | 45 |
| **Average** | **78.3** | **48.3** | **61.7** |

- **50 demos per task** (10 trajectories × 5 starting positions). Sorting has 10 per position, split 5 per color configuration (Sec. 4.1).
- Scoring is fine-grained partial credit: pick-place gives 0.5 for the grasp and 0.5 for the place (Sec. 4.1).
- The number of real evaluation trials per task is **not verified**; I did not find it stated.

*Real SO-101 Pick-Place-Lego (Table 4, single-task)*
- In-distribution: SmolVLA 90 vs ACT 70.
- Out-of-distribution positions: SmolVLA 50 vs ACT 40.
- SmolVLA was not pretrained on any SO-101 data.

*Effect of pretraining (Table 5, SO-100)*
- No robot pretraining, single-task: 40.
- No robot pretraining, multi-task: 51.7.
- With community pretraining: 78.3.

**Our recipe vs theirs [inferred arithmetic]**
- Samples seen in training:
  - Docs recipe: 20k × 64 = 1.28M samples.
  - Ours: 1k × 8 = 8k samples, **0.625% of the docs recipe** and 0.06% of the paper's 200k-step real-world recipe (if batch 64).
- Demos:
  - Theirs: 50 real teleop demos per task.
  - Ours: 44 sim episodes, all derived from 4 generated human clips. So our *independent* source count is 4, not 44.
- Evaluation:
  - Theirs: real-world partial-credit scores.
  - Ours: binary sim success.

## 4. Human video → robot

### 4a. EgoDex (Apple)

**Citation [read]**
- *EgoDex: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Large-Scale Egocentric Video*. Ryan Hoque, Peide Huang, David J. Yoon, Mouli Sivapurapu, Jian Zhang (Apple).
- arXiv 2505.11709 (v3, 9 Mar 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11709

**Data and training [read]**
- 829 hours of 1080p 30 Hz egocentric video with paired 3D hand and finger tracking, recorded on Apple Vision Pro.
- 338,000 episodes, 194 tabletop tasks, 90M frames, 2.0 TB (Sec. 3).
- Robot data: none.
- Compute: each model trained on 8× A100-80GB, 50,000 steps, batch 2048 (App. A.4).

**What they evaluate [read, Sec. 4 and 5]**
- Two benchmarks: *hand trajectory prediction* and *inverse dynamics*.
- Metric: best-of-K Euclidean distance in meters (average and final) on a 1% held-out test split.
- Example: a 500M decoder-only BC model reaches an average distance of 0.045 m and a final distance of 0.062 m, the same as the 200M model (Sec. 5).
- **No robot is trained or evaluated, and there are no robot success rates.** Robotics is listed as future use (Sec. 6).

### 4b. DexMV

**Citation [read]**
- *DexMV: Imitation Learning for Dexterous Manipulation from Human Videos*. Yuzhe Qin, Yueh-Hua Wu, Shaowei Liu, Hanwen Jiang, Ruihan Yang, Yang Fu, Xiaolong Wang.
- arXiv 2108.05877 (v1 12 Aug 2021). https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05877
- Venue: [not verified from the abs page].

**Data and method [read, Sec. 3 and 5]**
- Human videos were recorded with their own camera system at about 100 demos per hour.
- 3D hand and object poses are estimated from the video, then "demonstration translation" (retargeting) maps them to a simulated 30-DoF Adroit hand.
- 100 demos per object for Relocate (5 YCB objects), and 100 each for Pour and Place Inside.
- Sim only; robot teleop data is zero.

**Results [read, Table 1, Table 2, Fig. 7]**
- Relocate success is evaluated over 100 trials × 3 seeds, with IL methods built on top of TRPO RL.

| Relocate object | Mustard | Sugar box | Tomato can | Clamp | Mug |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DAPG (with DexMV demos) | 0.93 | 0.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| RL only (TRPO) | 0.06 | 0.00 | 0.67 | 0.51 | 0.49 |

- Demo scaling for SOIL on tomato can: 100 demos → 1.00 final success, 10 demos → 0.36 (Table 2).
- Pour success (%): DAPG 27.2, SOIL 3.5, GAIL+ 3.4 (Fig. 7).

**Relevance [inferred]**
- The pipeline is the most similar to ours in spirit: human video → hand pose → retarget → sim → policy.
- But DexMV uses *real* recorded human video, a dexterous hand, and RL-plus-IL.

### 4c. Phantom

**Citation [read]**
- *Phantom: Training Robots Without Robots Using Only Human Videos*. Marion Lepert, Jiaying Fang, Jeannette Bohg (Stanford).
- arXiv 2503.00779 (v2, 28 May 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00779 ; project page https://phantom-human-videos.github.io/

**Method [read, Sec. 3]**
- Third-person RGBD human videos using a pinch grasp.
- HaMeR hand pose becomes the end-effector action: the midpoint of the thumb and index tips, plus an orientation fit and the gripper width.
- The human arm is inpainted and a rendered robot is overlaid.
- The policy is Diffusion Policy with **zero robot data**, deployed zero-shot on a real Franka or Kinova.

**Data [read, Sec. 4]**
- 250 to 350 human video demos per task for the in-distribution tasks.
- 950 demos across many scenes for the out-of-distribution sweep task.

**Results [read]**
- In-distribution, 25 rollouts each (Table 1), Phantom (Hand Inpaint):
  - Pick/Place Book 0.92, Stack Cups 0.72, Tie Rope 0.64, Rotate Box 0.72, Grasp Brush 0.88.
- Baselines:
  - "Red Line", the EgoMimic-style edit: 0.0 on every task.
  - Vanilla (no editing): 0.0 on every task.
- Out-of-distribution sweep (Table 2): 0.72 outdoor lawn, 0.84 indoor lounge, 0.64 lounge with an OOD surface.
- Human vs robot demos (App., Table 6):
  - 50 teleop demos → 52%; 50 human demos → 44%.
  - 100 teleop → 88%; 100 human → 64%.
  - 300 human → 84%.
- The abstract headline is "up to 92%".

## 5. MimicGen family (sim data multiplication from a few human teleop demos)

### 5a. MimicGen

**Citation [read]**
- *MimicGen: A Data Generation System for Scalable Robot Learning using Human Demonstrations*. Ajay Mandlekar, Soroush Nasiriany, Bowen Wen, Iretiayo Akinola, Yashraj Narang, Linxi Fan, Yuke Zhu, Dieter Fox (NVIDIA, UT Austin).
- arXiv 2310.17596 (26 Oct 2023); CoRL 2023 (per the project page https://mimicgen.github.io/).

**Scale [read, Abstract]**
- Over 50K demos across 18 tasks from about 200 human demos.
- Per task, it is usually **10 source demos → 1,000 generated** (Fig. 4 caption).

**Mechanism [read, Sec. 4]**
1. Parse each source demo into **object-centric subtask segments**.
2. For a new scene, pick a source segment for each subtask.
3. Transform the end-effector target poses so their pose *relative to the subtask's object* is preserved: T_W^{C't} = T_W^{O'0} (T_W^{O0})^-1 T_W^{Ct}.
4. Add a linear interpolation segment from the current end-effector pose.
5. Execute the segment with the source gripper actions.
6. **Keep the demo only if the task succeeds.**

**Data-generation success rates [read]**
- Yes, reported. The "data generation rate" is in App. P.
- Sim examples: Object Cleanup D0 29.5%, Three Piece Assembly D0 35.6%, Coffee D2 27.7%, Gear Assembly D1 8.2%.
- Generation rate is **not** strongly correlated with policy success (Sec. 6).
- Real robot: 82.3% generation rate for Stack (243 attempts) and 52.1% for Coffee (192 attempts) (Sec. 6.4).

**Policy results [read]**
- Sim (image BC-RNN, 10 source demos vs 1,000 MimicGen demos):
  - Square: 11.3% → 90.7%.
  - Threading: 19.3% → 98.0%.
  - Three Piece Assembly: 1.3% → 82.0%.
  - Stack: 26.0% → 100%.
  - Pick Place: 0.0% → 50.7%.
- 200 MimicGen demos perform comparably to 200 human demos (Fig. 4).
- Real Franka, 50 evaluations each:
  - Stack: 36%, vs 0% for the source-only agent.
  - Coffee: 14%, vs 0% for the source-only agent.

**Relevance [inferred]**
- Our "4 clips re-anchored to 44 sim episodes" step is conceptually MimicGen-like: object-relative re-targeting to new cube poses.
- MimicGen is the right citation for that step.
- Unlike MimicGen, our source demos are generated human videos, not teleop.
- We also should state our own replay acceptance rate, which is the analogue of MimicGen's data generation rate.

### 5b. DexMimicGen

**Citation [read]**
- *DexMimicGen: Automated Data Generation for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation via Imitation Learning*. Zhenyu Jiang, Yuqi Xie, Kevin Lin et al., Ajay Mandlekar, Linxi Fan, Yuke Zhu.
- arXiv 2410.24185 (31 Oct 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24185

**Results [read]**
- 21K demos generated from 60 source human demos (Abstract).
- Sim, image policies trained on 1,000 generated trajectories vs source demos (Table I):
  - Threading: 1.3 → 69.3.
  - Can Sorting: 0.7 → 97.3.
  - Drawer Cleanup: 0.7 → 76.0.
  - Piece Assembly: 3.3 → 80.7.
- Real humanoid can sorting (Sec. VI-D), real-to-sim-to-real via a digital twin:
  - 4 source demos → 40 generated demos.
  - Diffusion Policy on generated data: **90% success**, vs 0% using only the human demos.
  - Evaluation: 10 trials each for red and blue cups.

## 6. Video-generation models used to produce robot behavior or training data

### 6a. Gen2Act (the closest to "text-to-video human demo → robot")

**Citation [read]**
- *Gen2Act: Human Video Generation in Novel Scenarios enables Generalizable Robot Manipulation*. Homanga Bharadhwaj, Debidatta Dwibedi, Abhinav Gupta, Shubham Tulsiani et al. (Google DeepMind, CMU).
- arXiv 2409.16283 (24 Sep 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16283 ; https://homangab.github.io/gen2act/

**Method [read, Sec. III and IV-B]**
- Pretrained VideoPoet, **not fine-tuned**, generates a *human* video from the scene image plus the task text.
- A closed-loop robot policy is **conditioned on** that generated video, with an auxiliary point-track prediction loss.
- The policy is trained on an **existing offline robot teleop dataset** (from RT-1, ref [1]), plus paired human videos from Vid2Robot, plus generated human videos paired to each robot demo's first frame.
- The generated human video is **not** converted into robot actions to become training data. It is a test-time conditioning signal.
- Robot: a real mobile manipulator running at 3 Hz.

**Results [read, Table I, success %]**

| Policy | MG | G | OTG | MTG | Avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RT-1 | 68 | 18 | 0 | 0 | 22 |
| Vid2Robot | 83 | 38 | 25 | 0 | 37 |
| **Gen2Act** | **83** | **67** | **58** | **30** | **60** |

- MG = mild generalization, G = standard, OTG = unseen object type, MTG = unseen motion type.
- Co-training with extra teleop data raises the average to 64 (Table III).
- The number of trials per task is [not verified].

### 6b. Dreamitate

**Citation [read]**
- *Dreamitate: Real-World Visuomotor Policy Learning via Video Generation*. Junbang Liang, Ruoshi Liu, Ege Ozguroglu, Sruthi Sudhakar, Achal Dave, Pavel Tokmakov, Shuran Song, Carl Vondrick.
- arXiv 2406.16862 (24 Jun 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16862

**Method [read]**
- A pretrained video diffusion model is fine-tuned on stereo videos of **humans using a 3D-printed tool**.
- At test time it generates a video, tracks the tool's 6D pose (Megapose), and the robot, holding the same tool, executes that trajectory.
- Video fine-tune: 768×448, batch 3 to 4, about 15 to 16K steps (App. Table 3).

**Data [read, Table 1]**
- Human demos: 371 (Rotation), 368 (Scooping), 356 (Sweeping), 727 (Push-Shape).
- Test trials: 40, 40, 40, 32.

**Results, real robot vs Diffusion Policy on the same data [read]**
- Rotation: 92.5% vs 55%.
- Scooping: 85% vs 55%.
- Sweeping: 92.5% vs 12.5%.
- Push-Shape: mIoU 0.731 vs 0.550.

### 6c. UniPi

**Citation [read]**
- *Learning Universal Policies via Text-Guided Video Generation*. Yilun Du, Mengjiao Yang, Bo Dai et al., Pieter Abbeel.
- arXiv 2302.00111; NeurIPS 2023 (stated in the PDF footer). https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00111

**Method [read]**
- A text-conditioned video model plans the future frames.
- An inverse dynamics model trained separately extracts the actions.

**Results [read]**
- Simulated combinatorial tabletop (Table 1):
  - UniPi: 59.1 / 53.2 (seen Place / Relation) and 60.1 / 46.1 (novel).
  - Best baseline: 19.4 or lower.
- Multitask environment (Table 3): 51.6 / 75.5 / 45.7 (Place Bowl / Pack Object / Pack Pair).
- Real Bridge data: only video-quality metrics plus a learned success *classifier* on generated videos (77.1% with pretraining, Table 4).
- **No real-robot policy success rate.**
